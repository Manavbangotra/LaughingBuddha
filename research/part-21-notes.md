## The four numbers this part is built on

| what | number | source |
|---|---|---|
| SWE-bench at launch | **1.96%** of 2,294 real issues resolved | `jimenez2023swebench` |
| "Solved" issues that are not | **29.6%** of plausible patches behave differently from the human patch; **28.6%** of those confirmed wrong; resolution rates inflated **~6.2 points** | `wang2025solvedcorrectly` |
| Memorisation | **76%** file-path accuracy from the issue text alone on SWE-bench repos vs **53%** elsewhere | `liang2025swebenchillusion` |

That last row is the most important thing in this part and the least known.

## Papers verified this pass

- `jimenez2023swebench` — SWE-bench (2310.06770, 10 Oct 2023, v3 11 Nov 2024, 7
  authors, ICLR 2024). **2,294 problems, 12 Python repositories**, graded by the
  repository's own tests. Best model at launch: **1.96%**. Requires coordinating
  changes across multiple functions, classes and files.

- `wang2025solvedcorrectly` — Are "Solved Issues" Really Solved? (2503.15223,
  19 Mar 2025, v2 9 Sep 2025, 3 authors). PatchDiff differential testing on three
  SOTA tools over SWE-bench Verified. **7.8%** of patches counted correct while
  failing the developer test suite; **29.6%** of plausible patches diverge
  behaviourally from ground truth; **28.6%** of those confirmed incorrect;
  reported rates inflated **~6.2 points**. *This is `verifier-sets-the-ceiling`
  measured in the best-instrumented coding setting there is.*

- `liang2025swebenchillusion` — The SWE-Bench Illusion (2506.12286, 14 Jun 2025,
  later version 1 Dec 2025, 3 authors). **76%** buggy-file identification from the
  issue description ALONE on SWE-bench repos vs **53%** on non-SWE-bench repos;
  function reproduction **35%** vs **18%** consecutive-5-gram. *The file-path
  result is the sharp one — locating a bug from prose without seeing the repo
  should not be possible at that rate.*

- `becker2025devproductivity` — Early-2025 AI on Developer Productivity
  (2507.09089, 12 Jul 2025, rev 25 Jul 2025, 4 authors). **RCT**, 16 experienced
  OSS developers, **246 tasks**, mature repos with ~5 years of prior experience,
  Cursor Pro + Claude 3.5/3.7 Sonnet. Forecast **−24%** time, self-estimate
  **−20%**, measured **+19%**. Economics experts predicted −39%, ML experts −38%.
  Authors examined 20 explanatory factors and found the slowdown robust.

  **The 39-point gap between self-report (−20%) and measurement (+19%) is the
  headline**, not the slowdown. It means practitioner testimony about these tools
  is not evidence about their effect.

  Scope caveat to state in the chapter: *experienced developers on mature codebases
  they know well.* That is one setting, and plausibly the least favourable one.

## The through-line

Part XX's finding transfers exactly: **coding is the domain with the strongest
verifier in the book — tests execute — and this part is about how far that gets
you.** The answer is: further than anywhere else, and `wang2025solvedcorrectly`
measures where it stops. A test suite is a partial specification, so passing it is
partial evidence, and 29.6% of passing patches do something else.

Then `becker2025devproductivity` supplies the other half: even where the tool
works, the *system* containing a human and a tool can be slower, and the humans in
it cannot tell.
